# RING OF FIRE — OREVOSH logo concept

1. **The mark.** One bold ring (the O of OREVOSH, a monoline circle the same weight as the lettering) with a single two-tongue lick of flame bursting out of its top-right; the flame starts inside the counter, crosses the band through a clean knock-out gap and flies free, so the ring is visibly *broken open* by fire. Pure geometry: a stroked circle, a masked cut and one cubic-bezier path — no font, no effects.
2. **Why it is OREVOSH.** The ring is the frame you step into: the outfit check, the score out of 10 that lands inside it, the loop of stylist → post → community → brand. The flame is the app's one currency — the "fire" reaction — so the logo *is* the moment a look catches fire; it is the first letter of the name, not a badge next to it, which is why the wordmark's first O is the mark itself. It borrows nothing: not a camera, not a musical note, not a heart.
3. **Small.** At 32 px the mark is a 22 px ring with a 12 px orange lick and a hairline gap — three shapes, two colours, no detail to lose; at 16 px it collapses gracefully to "ring + spark" and still reads as fire. The wordmark at 22 px height (≈15 px caps, 2.9 px strokes) reads OREVOSH in the phone header on dark and on cream; on light grounds the ring switches to the deeper pair (#7d5cff → #ff5c92) so it never washes out.
4. **Motion (the check button).** Idle: the ring sits in a dark disc with a faint lilac halo, the flame hidden. Tap: the ring draws itself clockwise from the bottom-left in ~450 ms (ease-out), and the instant the stroke reaches the top-right the lick flares up out of it with a 120 ms overshoot; while the AI thinks, the flame breathes (scale 0.92–1.06, 900 ms loop); when the verdict arrives the score drops into the ring and the flame flicks once toward the tip — the same motion the "fire" reaction uses on a post, so the check and the reaction are visibly one gesture.
5. **Colour logic.** The owner's palette is kept but each colour is given one job: near-black #0b0b0f is the stage; lilac #b39dff → rose #ff8fb1 runs diagonally across the ring (cool at top-left, warm at bottom-right — the stylist's calm meeting the community's warmth) and is the *only* gradient in the system; fire #ff6a2b (tipped to amber #ffa83a) is reserved for reactions and the flame alone, so orange anywhere in the app always means "this caught fire". The gradient deliberately does not put rose next to the flame: the lick sits on the mauve mid-point, keeping orange-vs-ring contrast crisp at favicon size.
