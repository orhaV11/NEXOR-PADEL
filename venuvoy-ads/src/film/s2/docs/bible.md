# PLAN, NO PLAN: series bible

## Premise
Two best friends travel to football matches abroad. Timo plans everything. Luna plans nothing. In every film one small trip problem meets his way and her way. One of them says "No". One cut later they are both doing it, and laughing.

## Tone
- Warm, sunny, playful. The teasing always ends with both laughing. Nobody is the fool, and each friend is right in turn.
- Friends, never a couple. They behave like brother and sister by choice: no romantic look or touch, ever.
- Football-trip true. Every situation is one a travelling fan knows. Properly loud at a goal, never aggressive.
- No irony, no subtext, no narrator, no brand before the last two seconds.

## The rules that keep every film clear
1. **The sentence first.** Before a script exists, the film is one sentence a twelve-year-old could say after watching once with the sound off. If the sentence needs "because" twice, the idea is too big.
2. **One big thing that turns round.** Each film is built on one large visible object or place whose meaning flips (in the pilot the yellow suitcase: first the problem, then the hero). The story can be followed by watching that thing alone.
3. **The want is in frame one**, as a gesture or a place (a pointing arm, floodlights, food held out), next to the thing that is in the way.
4. **One idea per shot**, at most two actions, held at least three seconds (two short clips of the same idea count together).
5. **Cause and effect are physical and in order** (slow uphill, fast downhill). Nothing is implied. Nothing must be remembered from another film.
6. **Only the story on screen.** The two friends, plus at most one local or the cat. Luna is always on the left, Timo always on the right.
7. **The "No" cut.** Once per film one friend offers their way with a big gesture, the other refuses with head and hands (it reads without sound), and a hard cut shows both already doing it.
8. **Every sound that matters has a picture double.** A roar is also streamers drifting or light on faces. A wrong clap is also two pairs of hands visibly out of step.
9. **Nothing to read in the picture.** The only text is the spoken line of the moment. Never two texts at once, no header. At most four short lines per film, and none of them carries the story: every cut is tested with all text covered.
10. **The last shot is the first shot turned round**, and the payoff is an action, never a caption and never the end card.
11. **Every film stands alone.** No "previously", no numbers, no cliffhanger.
12. **Three gates before anything is published.** Strip gate: the seven start pictures laid side by side tell the story with no text. Silent gate: the muted cut with captions covered is shown to a real viewer of 18 to 25 and to an adult with English as a second language; it goes out only if their sentence matches ours. Fault gate: every clip is checked edge to edge and on its last frame; a faulty clip is made again, never patched.

## Cast
**Timo, 23, the planner.** About 192 cm, thin, long legs, stands very straight. Dark brown skin, long oval face, round dark-brown eyes, thick straight eyebrows that tilt up in the middle when he worries, wide mouth, clean-shaven. Black hair in a short flat-top fade, one solid sculpted shape with sharp edges. No glasses. Always: a plain teal zip-up track jacket closed to the chin with a cream collar and cream cuffs (no stripes, no logo, plain back); cream straight trousers that end above the ankle; clean white trainers; a tiny square teal backpack worn high on both shoulders; a big chunky sunflower-yellow watch with a blank cream face on his left wrist. Trip scarf knotted once at the throat, both ends short and equal. He loves being early, points the way with his whole arm and moves in straight lines. When the plan breaks he freezes, says "No", and then enjoys it more than anyone. Neat means stylish here, not nerdy. Voice: soft, quick, clear light tenor, plain international English.

**Luna, 22, no plan.** About 158 cm (she reaches Timo's shoulder), sturdy, strong legs, weight on one hip, never quite still. East Asian features, warm light-tan skin, round face, full cheeks, a band of freckles across the nose, dark eyes that close into crescents when she laughs, short straight eyebrows, a wide grin with a small gap between the front teeth. Black blunt bob to the chin, slightly messy outline, the whole fringe coral; one solid sculpted shape. Always: an oversized coral retro football shirt with a cream collar and cream cuffs, completely plain front and back; baggy light-blue denim shorts to the knee; two sunflower-yellow socks pulled up; chunky cream trainers, scuffed, laces loose. Trip scarf loose round her neck, both ends hanging long. She says yes to everything, is always hungry and never worried, and drifts towards food, music and animals. Big laugh. Half of whatever she eats belongs to Timo. Voice: low, a little husky, loud, laughs in the middle of words, plain international English.

**Big Yellow, the suitcase.** A rounded sunflower-yellow hard box on four small deep-violet wheels, satin and not mirror-shiny. Its top is level with Luna's chest. Its flat lid is long enough for two people to sit one behind the other. Two wide raised ridges round its middle. One short fixed deep-violet grab handle on the front end; it never extends. No stickers, locks or letters. It has its own reference sheet like a cast member. What is inside is the running question of the series; when it opens in a later film, that is shown as a cut (closed, then already open). It never goes into a stadium: it waits at the bag hut.

**The ginger cat (working name Ref, never said on screen).** A fat ginger street cat with a cream belly and chin, short legs, a thick tail held up like a question mark and half-closed green-gold eyes. The calm local who watches the two visitors and is never impressed. Once or twice per film, never more.

**Locals.** One per film at most, in the same style, often the friends' own age: a food seller, a scarf seller, a fan who knows the clap, a waiter. The local is always the generous one and never the target of a joke.

## World and look
Stylised, not photoreal: collectible art-toy figures in streetwear on a hand-built miniature town in real sunlight. Adult proportions, matte skin, solid sculpted hair, real-fabric clothes with a coarse soft weave. One invented sunny hill town used for every trip: painted houses in cream, terracotta, mint and sky blue, cobbled streets, low walls, plain bunting. No letters, numbers or clocks anywhere. The stadium is only a soft cream bowl with four floodlight masts and plain flags, plus its sound; other fans are still, soft colour shapes holding scarves up. Near every ground stands the bag hut, a small striped hut with shelves of bags. Palette: cream base, teal for Timo, coral for Luna, sunflower yellow shared, deep violet as the darkest colour, no black and no grey. The trip scarf is one half teal and one half coral with a cream fringe and belongs to no club; the home colours of the town are cream and violet. Light: always sun, low and golden, always from screen left and a little in front of the faces. Camera: as if a third friend is filming, at eye level, close. Full-frame 9:16.

## Music theme
"Tick Tock Clap", made for the series: sunny afro-house with an amapiano bounce at exactly 120 beats a minute, built in separate layers. Timo is a tidy tick-tock on the beat. Luna is a lazy log drum with a cowbell that lands a little late. When they agree, both lock under a five-clap stand pattern (clap, clap, clap-clap-clap) and a rough three-note "oh-oh-oh" from three or four young voices. Tick-tock from frame one, tick-tock alone on the "No", the full groove on the hard cut, the voices on the turn, the last three claps on the card. Natural sound is as loud as the music.

## Caption style
Only the spoken line, nothing else. Bold rounded sans-serif, sentence case, cream letters on a soft deep-violet plate. One typeface, one size, one position in every film: centred about two thirds of the way down the frame, above the platform's own text and buttons and clear of the right edge. Two to six words. It appears on the first syllable and stays at least a second and a half. Never two texts at once, no header, no coloured words, no emoji, no pop effects. Start pictures are composed so that no face sits in the caption band.

## The shape of a film
22 to 30 seconds, at most seven shots, then a two-second card and a loop.
1. Frame one: the want and the thing in the way, both visible. No title, no logo.
2. It gets worse, and the cat sees it.
3. The offer and the "No".
4. Hard cut: both are doing it.
5. The turn: the one who refused enjoys it most.
6. The topper: one more picture laugh.
7. The payoff: the first picture turned round, one last short line, hold.

## What never happens
- Anything that must be read in the picture: signs, screens, chats, tickets, shirts with words.
- A header line, a narrator, a to-camera "hey guys", a slogan, an app, a phone, a price, "link in bio".
- A joke that lives only in a line, irony, a mock documentary, a joke that needs another film.
- Humiliation. Nobody loses: not a friend, not a local, not the fan who does not know the clap.
- Romance between Timo and Luna.
- Night, dim rooms, grey colour, middle-aged leads.
- Aggression, flares, rival fans as enemies. Alcohol as the joke. The word "soccer". Real clubs, badges, kits, stadiums, cities, players or brands. Slang of one country.
- More than the two friends plus one local or the cat in a frame. A crowd in focus, match action, the pitch itself (only its light on faces).
- An object passed from hand to hand on camera, precise hand work, mirrors. A hand-over is always a cut.
- A change of outfit, a second look, a patched ending, a post without the platform's AI label.

## End card
Two seconds on a plain warm cream card: the word VENUVOY in deep violet in the middle, the teal-and-coral trip scarf lying flat under it like an underline, and two small words below: "football trips". Sound: the last three claps, the final one ending dry. No logo animation, no app, no link. The card cuts straight back to frame one, so the film loops.

## Posting
The post text carries what the film does not say: "When your friend packs for one night." and "Plan or No Plan: which one are you?". Account line: "Two best friends. Football trips abroad." Never "tag your friend". Every post carries the AI label.
