# Brief: a NEW series for Venuvoy (everything new)

## What the client said about the first series ("To Be Confirmed"), translated from Hebrew
"Listen, this is simply bad. I understood nothing, I did not understand the plot. I want you to invest much more.
Give it a young, nice vibe, with a nice, cool atmosphere. I want different characters, everything new, and this
time without screw-ups."

## Why the first series failed (so nobody repeats it)
- It was a grave, deadpan mock documentary. The mood was serious, like true crime. Nobody on screen was having fun.
- The story was told through screenshots of a group chat, paperwork boards, rubber stamps, counters and a
  narrator. The viewer had to READ fast and to work out what was implied. Nothing simply happened in front of you.
- Jokes lived in subtext and in running gags that needed earlier episodes. A single film did not tell a story.
- The cast was mostly middle-aged, filmed at night or in dim rooms, speaking dry British understatement.
- A test viewer (an AI model that watches the film with sound) said: fast text, attention split between reading
  and listening, "serious and dramatic like a true-crime documentary", "I would not follow unless I liked this
  specific British comedy".
- Production slips: a cup that changed hands, a black band at the top of a shot, voices that changed between
  episodes, a closing line that had to be patched from another take.

## What Venuvoy is, and what the series is for
Venuvoy is an app that plans a football trip abroad from one typed sentence (match ticket, flight, hotel, the
day around the match). The series is NOT an advert: no app, no phone screen, no price, no slogan, no "download".
It builds a community of young football fans (18 to 30, international, most with English as a second language)
who travel, or dream of travelling, to matches abroad. The brand only signs the last second ("VENUVOY").

## What the new series must be
1. UNDERSTOOD AT ONCE. One film = one small story: someone wants something, something gets in the way, it pays
   off. A twelve-year-old who watched once WITH THE SOUND OFF must be able to retell it in one sentence. Told by
   faces, places and actions in front of the camera, not by text screens, not by implication.
2. YOUNG, WARM, COOL. People in their early twenties you would like to travel with. Daylight, colour, energy,
   music you would add to a playlist. Funny through situations and reactions, never through irony at the
   viewer's expense. Nobody is humiliated; the feeling at the end is "I want a trip like that with my friends".
3. NEW. New characters, new look, new format, new title. Nothing from the first series returns.
4. FOOTBALL-TRIP TRUE. The situations are ones that travelling fans recognise (the early flight, the friend who
   packs for a month, the hostel, finding the stadium, the scarf seller, the ticket check, the chant you do not
   know, the train back). No real club, league, player, stadium, city rivalry or brand. Never "soccer".
5. SIMPLE ENGLISH. Short everyday sentences that a second-language viewer follows; at most about 10 words in a
   spoken line; no slang of one country. A film must also work with no words at all.
6. Vertical 9:16, 20 to 40 seconds. Each film stands alone; a light thread across films is welcome.

## Production reality (a script that breaks these cannot be made well)
- Every shot is an AI-generated video clip of about 4 to 8 seconds that starts from a designed still picture.
  Recurring characters stay the same person because each shot starts from their reference pictures.
- Works well: one or two people in frame; a face reacting; walking, running, hugging, cheering, eating,
  dragging a suitcase; a short spoken line with the lips in sync (one speaker per shot is safest, two people
  exchanging one short line each is possible); natural sound; a simple camera move.
- Risky, avoid or keep away from the centre: readable text inside the picture (signs, screens, shirts with
  words or badges: they come out garbled), hands doing something precise with an object, an object passed from
  hand to hand, fast sports action, a crowd in focus, more than three people who all must stay consistent,
  mirrors, long continuous choreography.
- On-screen text added in the edit: big friendly captions of what is said, at most ONE short line at the top
  that states the situation (optional), and the end card. Nothing else to read.
- Music is made for the series (a theme the films share). Sound matters as much as picture.
- The look may be photoreal live action OR a designed, stylised look (for example warm 3D animation): choose
  what serves a young, cool feeling and hides the weaknesses of generated video. Say which and why.
