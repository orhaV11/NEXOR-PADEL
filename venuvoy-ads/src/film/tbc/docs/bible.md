# The series bible of "To Be Confirmed" (the showrunner's, recovered from the session record)

1. PREMISE. Last season eight friends planned one trip abroad to watch their club. Two people arrived, and neither was in the chat. This season they are doing it properly. A grave documentary follows the group chat 'Away trip – who's in?' from the morning the dates come out to match day. It continues 'Danny Left the Chat', which becomes 'last season'; a new viewer needs none of it.
2. ENGINE. Three clocks that never agree: the date of the match (decided later, by somebody else), the tickets (which require a history of buying tickets) and the flights (which must be booked before either). Everything gets booked. The match stays 'to be confirmed'. Each episode moves exactly one clock, states one finding and tops it once, so it is complete on its own.
3. PEOPLE. Maya organises and has paid for everyone. Jo has never said yes or no. Vic is in, hundred percent, and holds every ticket on his phone. Lee compares until the flights are gone. Marcus reports each time the kick-off moves. 'Sydney' (text only for now) lives twenty-two hours away and answers at two in the morning his time. Danny is typing. Danny's grandmother is not in the chat and is always one step ahead without trying.
4. VOICE. The narrator of 'Danny': plain documentary English, no slang from any country, never 'soccer'. He reports and never comments, calls people 'the organiser' or 'the subject', and measures in hours, flights and days. The people speak in short statements of fact with the feeling left out. Nothing is explained; a thud or a silence is allowed to be the punchline.
5. WHAT NEVER HAPPENS. Nobody mugs, winks or has a catchphrase. Nobody is the fool: not the visitor, not the local, not the person who is owed money. No drink jokes, no prices, no real club, league, player or competition, no borders-as-politics, no crowd in focus, no piano. The scoreline is never the punchline. Two cast members never speak to each other in one frame; they answer each other in the cut.
6. THE OPENING. Frame 0 is a face already mid-expression, never a slate, a logo, a fade or a chat screen. One line of at most seven words is on screen by 0.3 s in the large caption style, so the first two seconds work with no sound. The first laugh or open question lands by second 3. The day-count tag ('96 DAYS BEFORE*' with a small '*to be confirmed') arrives after second 2 and the wordmark not before second 3.
7. THE FURNITURE. The chat is always seen from Maya's phone, with 'Danny is typing…' at the bottom of every chat screen. One red rubber stamp with a thud per episode, on something that should be certain (TO BE CONFIRMED; ARRIVED? in the 'Elsewhere' shorts). The corner chip OWE MAYA: 6 arrives in Episode 2 and never changes. Every spoken word is burned in, the narrator in a second style as large as the speakers'. Between numbered episodes run two cheap slots in the same world: 'Elsewhere' (one existing clip, one verdict) and 'In the Chat' (the chat alone).
8. THE SIGN-OFF. The last 1.8 seconds are always Danny's grandmother sipping her tea with a one-line status ('NOTHING BOOKED.', 'FREE ON MONDAY.') and the line 'Next episode: Thursday. Confirmed.' It is the only confirmed date in the series.
9. THE BRAND RULE. Venuvoy signs and never speaks: a small letter-spaced wordmark in the top corner from second 3 and the closing line. No app, no screen, no phone alert, no tagline, no button, no URL, no price, in the video or in the caption. The grandmother's two product-shaped lines from the old film are not used; nobody says how she does it. The one product sentence lives in the profile bio. Every post carries the platform's AI label and the documentary is openly fiction.
10. THE COMMUNITY MECHANIC. The Record. From Episode 4 the study keeps a record of matches that moved after the flights were booked: the day you booked for, the day it moved to, the reason given, with no club names. Real entries only, numbered from 001, read back gravely in later episodes with first name and city once the person agrees. The poll under Episode 1 also stays open ('Options other than yes and no are being recorded.'). Nobody is asked to like, tag, share or follow, and comments are answered in the narrator's voice or not at all.
